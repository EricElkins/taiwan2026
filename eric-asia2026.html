Your realistic work windows

A Denver workday (9–5) lands in the dead of your night everywhere on this trip. The overlap that actually works is early Denver morning = your evening. Take calls over dinner; block anything past mid-morning Denver.

🇹🇼 Taiwan
Your evening 7:00–11:00 PM = Denver 5:00–9:00 AM. A 7–8 AM Denver call is 9–10 PM for you — easy after dinner. Anything at/after Denver 10 AM is past midnight your time.
🇯🇵 Japan
Your evening 8:00 PM–midnight = Denver 5:00–9:00 AM. Or catch late-afternoon Denver (4–5 PM) first thing your morning (7–8 AM). Midday-Denver meetings mean a middle-of-the-night call — avoid.
🇰🇷 Korea
Same as Japan through Oct 31 (evening 8–12 = Denver 5–9 AM). From Nov 1, shift one hour later: your 9 PM–1 AM = Denver 5–9 AM.
Wi-Fi for a surprise meeting: every stay on the Japan/Korea leg has in-unit Wi-Fi and a washer, and the Taiwan hotels all have solid Wi-Fi — so you can take a call from the room. If you're out, the rule that's held up: department-store cafés and hotel lobbies over random street coffee shops (real bandwidth, a quiet corner). Keep the room as the fallback for anything that matters.

😴 Jet-Lag Plan — Timeshifter-Style

Built for your in-between clock (bed ~11:30 PM, up ~7 AM) using light, caffeine and sleep timing — no melatonin, with your ashwagandha / magnesium glycinate / L-theanine wind-down folded in. Two shifts matter: a 10-hour delay going to Taiwan (staying up later — suits your night-owl trip), and a harder 8-hour advance coming home. The tiny +1h to Japan and 0h Japan↔Korea you won't feel.

⚠️

Modeled guidance, not medical advice — the Timeshifter app itself will give minute-precise timing if you want it. On the diazepam: only if your doctor prescribed it, and never combine it with alcohol — both are sedatives and the mix is genuinely dangerous. Skip the in-flight wine if you take it.

🧳 Packing — One Carry-On, Three Climates

🌡

27 days, no checked bag, ~40°F swing. Taiwan 75–87°F humid → Japan mid-50s–70°F → Seoul down to ~43°F. Strategy is layering: the tee that works in a Taipei night market goes under everything else in Seoul. Every Japan/Korea stay (and your Taiwan hotels) has laundry, so pack ~5–6 days and wash per leg.

👕 Clothing

  • 5–6 quick-dry tees (carry the whole trip; worn alone in Taiwan, layered up north)
  • 2–3 lightweight button-downs / collared shirts (open over a tee in Taiwan; layer in Japan/Korea; doubles for dinners & museums)
  • 2 pairs pants (one casual, one nicer) + 1 pair shorts for Taiwan/Kaohsiung
  • Warm mid-layer — packable fleece or light down (the Japan/Korea layer; don't skip)
  • Packable rain shell / windbreaker (Taipei typhoon-tail + autumn showers) — your outer layer up north
  • Scarf + light gloves for the cold Seoul nights
  • One smart-casual outfit for the birthday dinners
  • 5–6 quick-dry underwear + socks (sink-wash backup)

👟 Shoes

  • Broken-in walking shoes, ideally slip-on — shoes come off constantly at ryokan, temples, machiya & teamLab
  • Thin sandals/flip-flops (Taiwan hot springs, hotel, onsen)
  • Nice socks for teamLab (shoes off) — at Planets you wade through water, so quick-dry or roll-up trousers help

🔌 Tech & power

  • Adapters: Taiwan & Japan are Type A (same as US — no adapter); Korea is Type C/F — bring one compact adapter for the Korea leg
  • Power bank (10,000 mAh+), universal charging cable, headphones
  • Laundry kit — a few detergent sheets covers every in-unit washer

📄 Docs & money

  • Passport valid 6+ months past Nov 2; US citizens visa-free for all three (Taiwan/Japan/Korea 90 days)
  • Visit Japan Web — register before flying, save the QR for immigration/customs
  • No-foreign-fee cards; carry some cash (NT$, ¥, ₩ all still useful at markets/shrines)
  • Travel insurance card + policy number saved in your phone (details in the dossier)

📱 Practical Setup

Phone & data

All three countries are covered by the major US carriers — passes activate on first use abroad (the clock starts then, so don't buy early). Install/sign into your carrier app on home Wi-Fi before you fly. If your plan's international data is thin, a 3-country e-SIM (Airalo/Holafly, ~$20–40 for the stretch) is the clean alternative. Use FaceTime/WhatsApp/Wi-Fi calling for calls home and save the paid pass for days you need a real call.

Transit cards

Taiwan EasyCard (TPE airport / any 7-Eleven) · Japan Suica or Pasmo (add to Apple Wallet — works on all rail + convenience stores) · Korea T-money (any CU/GS25; add to Wallet). Load each at the start of its leg.

Apps to grab before you go

Translation: Google Translate (offline JP + KR packs, camera mode) · Papago (best for Korean). Transit: Japan Travel by NAVITIME (Japan) · KakaoMap or Naver Map (Google transit is weak in Korea). Taxis: GO (Japan) · Kakao T (Korea — essential in Busan & Seoul). Food: Tabelog (Japan) · CatchTable + Naver (Korea). Messaging: LINE (Japan) · KakaoTalk (Korea).
