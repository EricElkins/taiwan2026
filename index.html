City by City

🍜

The guiding principle: Two adventurous eaters, no restrictions, maximum curiosity. Taiwan is one of the great food destinations on earth — night markets, pre-dawn soup spots, hole-in-the-wall noodle shops, and a fine dining scene that largely goes unnoticed by the Western press. Eat at all hours. Joseph's art history background unlocks the museum layer, which is world-class and deeply underrated in Taiwan specifically.

Everything below is grouped by city, in trip order — where to stay, what to do, and the daily rhythm together. No Joan, no 10pm bedtimes: the day-by-day flow is loose by design. Adjust freely based on how late the night before went.

🎨 Do & eat

🎨 Art: Chimei Museum — one of the great surprises in Asia. Private collection: European Old Masters (Rubens, Poussin, Boucher), natural history, medieval arms and armor, and one of the world's largest violin collections. Free admission. 30 min from central Tainan by taxi. Give Joseph the full morning. Also: National Museum of Taiwan History for indigenous culture and the Dutch / Qing / Japanese colonial layers.

🏛 Historic Tainan: Anping Old Fort (Dutch, 1624 — Taiwan's oldest European structure). Confucius Temple (oldest in Taiwan, 1665). Hayashi Department Store (1932 Japanese-era, beautifully restored). Walk Shennong Street at night — herb shops, craft studios, lanterns, old atmosphere.

🍜 Food — this is the point of coming to Tainan: Coffin bread (棺材板 guāncái bǎn — thick-fried toast stuffed with cream sauce and seafood; the name means "coffin board"). Dawn milkfish soup (虱目魚) — open from 6am, eaten at sunrise with rice. Tainan beef soup (牛肉湯) — raw beef slices scalded tableside by boiling broth; Eric's kind of dish. Dù Xiaoyuè for peddler's noodles (擔仔麵 dànzǎi miàn) — the original. Shrimp rolls (蝦捲). All of Zhongzheng Road and Guohua Street market.

🌙 Nightlife: Smaller than Taipei but real — craft beer and cocktail spots on Zhongzheng and around Anping. Slower pace is the point; a single great bar and nowhere to be is very Tainan.

📅 Daily rhythm

🌅 Dawn
Milkfish soup stall — open from 6am. This is the move. Eat at sunrise, walk it off through the Confucius Temple area before the heat builds. Then Anping Fort.
☀️ Midday
Chimei Museum — give Joseph the time he needs. Hayashi Department Store for craft browsing. Shennong Street in the late afternoon.
🌆 Evening
Eat your way through Zhongzheng Road and Guohua Street. Coffin bread. Beef soup. Peddler's noodles. Shrimp rolls. Dumplings. In whatever order the stalls present themselves. Bar on Anping afterward.

🎲 Choose Your Own Adventure — Optional Detours

Unlike the Japan/Korea trip, this itinerary has room to breathe. Below are three detours that could replace a city leg, extend a stay, or happen entirely on the fly. Nothing is booked. Everything is possible. Pick one and go.

🏙 Taichung — Josh's Recommendation
Taiwan's third city · art · Fengjia Night Market · one legendary bar
~1h from Taipei by THSR
~45min from Tainan by THSR
1–2 nights ideal

Taichung sits almost exactly between Taipei and Tainan on the High Speed Rail — making it a natural overnight detour on the way south rather than a separate mission. Josh knows his stuff, and the case for Taichung is genuinely compelling: it's grown into one of Taiwan's most interesting cultural cities, with a serious art scene, a café culture that rivals Taipei, and the largest night market on the island.

🎨 For Joseph: The National Taichung Theater is reason enough to stop. Designed by Toyo Ito (of Sendai Mediatheque fame) — a radical, curving, cave-like structure that was nearly impossible to build and even harder to describe. Widely considered one of the most extraordinary buildings in Asia. Also: Rainbow Village (a retired soldier painted his entire village in folk murals to save it from demolition — now a landmark), and the National Museum of Natural Science.

🍜 Food: Fengjia Night Market — the biggest and arguably best night market in Taiwan. More food, more stalls, more chaos than Raohe or Shilin. Also: Taichung has a serious breakfast culture and a strong independent café and cocktail scene — the city punches well above its weight for nightlife.

How to plug this in: Take THSR from Taipei on Oct 13 morning, stop in Taichung for 1–2 nights, continue to Tainan on Oct 14 or 15. Compresses Tainan to 1–2 nights instead of 3 — manageable if you've already done Taipei food deeply. Or skip Tainan partially and go Taipei → Taichung (1 night) → Kaohsiung (2 nights), using a day trip or half-day from Kaohsiung for Tainan's key dishes.
🏔 Taroko Gorge — The Wild Card
Eastern Taiwan · marble canyon · one of Asia's most dramatic landscapes
~2h from Taipei by train
Base city: Hualien
1–2 nights minimum

Taroko is a marble canyon on Taiwan's east coast cut by a river over millennia — sheer cliff walls, turquoise water, suspension bridges, tunnels blasted through rock, and one of the most visually staggering national parks in Asia. It is emphatically not the Taiwan that most people picture, which is exactly why it's worth seeing.

🎨 For Joseph: The Taiwanese indigenous Truku people have lived in this canyon for centuries — their weaving, woodworking, and ceremonial culture are documented at the park visitor center and in Hualien's cultural spaces. The landscape itself, from an art history standpoint, informed an entire generation of Taiwanese landscape painters.

🥾 Do: Shakadang Trail (riverside canyon path, easy, stunning), Swallow Grotto (the most dramatic section of the gorge road — cliff faces riddled with holes, rock formations overhead), Baiyang Waterfall Trail (ends in a waterfall you walk behind), and Qingshui Cliffs (Pacific Ocean on one side, vertical cliff faces on the other — breathtaking from the coastal highway).

🍜 Hualien food: The city is famous for scallion pancakes (蔥油餅), pork rice, and the freshest Pacific seafood you'll eat in Taiwan — the east coast fish market is extraordinary by 6am.

How to plug this in: Add 1–2 nights to the start of the trip — arrive Taipei Oct 9, take the Puyuma Express east to Hualien Oct 10 or 11, return to Taipei Oct 11 or 12, then continue south as planned. Or use Taroko as a bookend: Hualien is reachable from Taipei before flying out Oct 18, though the logistics get tight. Best treated as a Taipei extension, not a southern add-on.
🌅 Sun Moon Lake — The Scenic Slowdown
Central Taiwan mountains · cycling · tea · indigenous culture · famous for a reason
~1.5h from Taichung by bus
~2.5h from Taipei by bus
1 night sweet spot

Taiwan's most famous lake sits at 748 meters in the mountains of Nantou County. The name comes from the shape: one half resembles a sun, the other a crescent moon. It is not a secret — it draws enormous Taiwanese domestic tourism — but it earned its reputation. Morning fog on the water, aboriginal villages around the shore, cycling the full 33km loop on a rented electric bike, and the surrounding tea plantations that produce some of Taiwan's best high-mountain oolongs.

🎨 For Joseph: The Formosan Aboriginal Culture Village is adjacent — 14 distinct indigenous cultures represented in reconstructed villages, performing arts, and a genuinely serious ethnographic collection. Also: the lakeside Wenwu Temple is one of the most architecturally grand temple complexes in Taiwan, set directly above the water.

🍜 Eat and drink: The Thao indigenous people (the lake's original inhabitants) have their own cuisine — grilled freshwater fish, wild boar, mochi made from freshly pounded rice. Sun Moon Lake tea (紅茶, hóngchá — a red/black oolong) is famous; every lakeside café brews it. Don't miss it.

How to plug this in: Pairs naturally with a Taichung stop — Taichung → Sun Moon Lake by bus (1.5h), spend the afternoon and night, return to Taichung or continue directly south by bus to Tainan HSR station. Works as a one-night detour without meaningfully disrupting the route. Also possible as a standalone day trip from Taipei if you don't want an overnight.

Packing — Two Very Different Trips

Joseph flies home from Taiwan on Oct 18. Eric continues through Japan and Korea to Nov 2. One of these packing jobs is easy; the other is a 24-day, three-country, 40°F-swing logistics puzzle. Both fit in a carry-on.

🧳 Joseph — Taiwan Only

Oct 9 – 18 · 9 nights · one climate: hot & humid
🌡 75–87°F the whole time
Pack light, pack cool

👕 Clothing

  • 6–7 quick-dry t-shirts — cotton holds sweat and stinks fast in Taiwan humidity. Pack enough tops and laundry becomes optional on a 9-night trip.
  • 1–2 lightweight button-downs — worn open over a tee; doubles as the "nicer" layer for museums and sit-down dinners.
  • 1 pair of lightweight pants + 2 pairs of shorts — shorts are the daily uniform; pants for nicer dinners and aggressively air-conditioned museums.
  • 1 packable rain jacket — it's typhoon-tail season. This is for rain, not warmth: it will not drop below ~70°F. No cold-weather anything.
  • 7+ pairs quick-dry underwear and socks — sink-wash as backup.
  • Broken-in walking shoes + thin sandals — 20,000-step days; sandals for hot springs, the beach, and the hotel.

🔌 Tech & money

  • No plug adapter needed — Taiwan uses Type A, same as the US.
  • Taiwan-only e-SIM — ~$10–15 for 10 days of data (Airalo or similar). Set it up before flying.
  • Power bank — night-market nights are long. 10,000 mAh is plenty.
  • EasyCard at TPE baggage claim or any 7-Eleven — all MRT, buses, convenience stores. Load NT$500–1,000.
  • NT$3,000–5,000 cash (~$100–150) from the airport ATM. Cards work at hotels and sit-down restaurants; cash everywhere else.

📄 Documents & health

  • Passport valid 6+ months past Oct 18, 2026 — US citizens get 90 days visa-free.
  • Travel insurance with trip interruption (typhoon tail risk is real, see Flights section).
  • Blister plasters, antacid, sunscreen, prescriptions in original packaging.

🌏 Eric — Taiwan → Japan → Korea

Oct 7 – Nov 2 · 24 days · three countries · one carry-on
🌡 87°F Taipei → 45°F Seoul
Layering is the whole game
🌡

24 days, no checked bags, three countries, 40°F temperature swing. Taiwan (Oct 9–18): 75–87°F, humid. Japan (Oct 18 – ~Oct 26): mid-60s°F. Korea (Oct 26–Nov 2): 45–60°F, can feel cold. The strategy is layering — the same clothes that work in a Taipei night market work in Seoul with everything else piled on top.

🧺

Laundry strategy: Japan and Korea both have coin laundry everywhere (often in the basement of your hotel), plus most places have a washer. Pack for 5–6 days max and do one wash per leg. This is how you travel 24 days in a carry-on.

👕 Clothing — the core list

  • 5–6 quick-dry t-shirts — these carry the whole trip. Moisture-wicking; cotton holds sweat and stinks fast in Taiwan humidity. Worn alone in Taiwan, layered under everything else in Korea.
  • 2–3 lightweight button-down or collared shirts — worn open over a tee in Taiwan; alone or under a jacket in Japan/Korea. Doubles as a "nicer" option for sit-down dinners and museums.
  • 2 pairs of pants — one casual (travel/jogger-style), one slightly nicer. Leave the jeans; they take a day to dry and add a pound of weight.
  • 1 pair of shorts — for Taiwan and Kaohsiung beach days. Leave them in a locker at the airport before Japan if you want to reclaim the space.
  • 1 packable rain jacket — wears as a windbreaker, packs to nothing. Essential for Taipei typhoon tail risk and for Japan rain. This is your outermost layer for mild-cold Japan days.
  • 1 mid-layer — fleece or lightweight down — the Japan/Korea layer. A packable down jacket (~$60 at any outdoor store) adds almost no weight and keeps you warm through Seoul in November. Do not skip this.
  • 5–6 pairs of quick-dry underwear — wash in the sink, dry overnight. Merino wool if you can swing it; synthetic otherwise.
  • 5–6 pairs of socks — you'll need more than you think. Add one pair of thin liner socks for blister prevention during 20,000-step days.
  • 1 pair of broken-in walking shoes — your primary footwear for the whole trip. Must be tested before you leave; this is not the time for new shoes.
  • Flip-flops or lightweight sandals — essential for Taiwan (hot springs, beach, temple courtyards) and Japan onsen. Pack the thinnest pair you own.
  • One warm hat and lightweight gloves — for Korea in late October/November nights. Packable, barely takes space.

🔌 Tech & Power

  • Plug adapter: Taiwan and Japan both use Type A (same as US) — no adapter needed for either. Korea uses Type C (European two-round-pin) — bring one compact travel adapter for the Korea leg, or buy one for a few dollars at Incheon airport.
  • Phone plan: An international e-SIM covering all three countries is the cleanest approach. Get one before you fly — options like Airalo, Holafly, or SimOptions typically run $20–40 for 3 weeks of data. Your US carrier's international day-pass works too but adds up fast over 24 days.
  • Power bank — long market and temple days drain phones constantly. 10,000 mAh is enough; 20,000 mAh if you want peace of mind.
  • Headphones — long trains, long flights. Joseph knows.
  • Universal charging cable — USB-C if your devices support it; covers most modern phones and laptops.

💳 Transit Cards — get all three

  • Taiwan EasyCard (悠遊卡): Buy at TPE airport baggage claim or any 7-Eleven. Works on all MRT, buses, some taxis, and 7-Eleven purchases across the island. Load NT$500–1,000 to start.
  • Japan Suica or Pasmo IC card: Get at Narita or Haneda airport. Works on virtually all rail and metro in Japan (JR, Tokyo Metro, regional trains), plus convenience stores and vending machines. Load ¥3,000–5,000.
  • Korea T-money: Buy at Incheon airport or any convenience store (CU, GS25). Works on Seoul metro, city buses, and some taxis. Load ₩30,000–50,000.

📄 Documents & Money

  • Passport valid at least 6 months past Nov 2, 2026. US citizens: no visa required for Taiwan (90 days), Japan (90 days), or South Korea (90 days). Confirm entry requirements for your specific passport before departure.
  • Credit cards with no foreign transaction fees — Visa and Mastercard accepted widely at hotels and restaurants. Cash is still king at night markets and smaller spots everywhere.
  • Local cash, leg by leg: Taiwan: NT$3,000–5,000 (~$100–150) at TPE airport ATM. Japan: ¥20,000–30,000 (~$130–200). Korea: ₩100,000–200,000 (~$75–150). Airport ATMs in all three countries are reliable and give good rates.
  • Travel insurance with medical + trip interruption coverage. Non-negotiable for a 24-day trip. October still carries typhoon tail risk in Taiwan and Japan.

💊 Health & Comfort

  • Any prescriptions in original packaging — pharmacies are abundant in all three countries but matching formulations takes time you won't want to spend
  • Blister plasters — you will blow through these. Take more than you think you need.
  • Antacid/stomach relief — you will test your limits at every market. This is good. Have the backup.
  • Sunscreen — especially for Taiwan and any outdoor Kaohsiung or Kenting days. Japan and Korea in Oct are milder but you'll still want it.
  • Small first-aid kit: antiseptic wipes, bandages, ibuprofen, antihistamine

🧳 Bag strategy

  • 1 overhead carry-on (22" × 14" × 9" max — check your airline) for clothes, shoes, and gear
  • 1 personal item backpack (fits under seat) for daily use: phone charger, power bank, passport, camera, snacks, a layer. This bag is your day bag in every city — bring one you'd want to carry for 10 hours.
  • Buying things: You will buy food, books, and probably a few things at markets. Japan especially makes this dangerous. Factor in that you'll need room on the way home, or plan to ship a box — Japan Post's shipping boxes from any post office are easy and reliable (~$30–50 for a medium box to the US).
